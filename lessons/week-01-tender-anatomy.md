## 3. Core concepts

### 3.1 Procurement instrument types

| Type | Full name | Purpose | Typical outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RFI** | Request for Information | Market research; buyer is still scoping | No award; informs a later tender |
| **RFQ** | Request for Quotation | Low-value, defined goods/services | Quick award, often lowest compliant price |
| **RFP** | Request for Proposal | Complex/professional services where *approach* matters | Functionality-weighted award |
| **RFB / open tender** | Request for Bid | Formal advertised competitive bid | Award on price + preference after responsiveness |
| **EOI** | Expression of Interest | Pre-qualification / shortlisting | Shortlist invited to a later RFP/RFB |

The eTenders portal lets you filter opportunities by these types — use the filters deliberately so you study one category at a time.

**Consequence of type:** an RFQ rarely rewards good writing; an RFP is won on writing. Knowing which is which tells you where your labour is worth money.

### 3.2 The standard skeleton of a pack

Most SA public tender packs contain, in some arrangement:

1. **Invitation / advertisement** — tender number, description, closing date/time, briefing details, contact person, CIDB requirement if construction.
2. **Tender notice and conditions of tender** — the rules of the competition (the "how to bid" law of this tender).
3. **Returnable schedules** — every form, declaration and schedule you must hand back.
4. **Standard bidding forms** — SBD family (national/provincial) or MBD family (municipal).
5. **Scope of work / terms of reference / specification** — what is actually being bought.
6. **Evaluation criteria** — eligibility, responsiveness, functionality thresholds, points system.
7. **Pricing schedule / BOQ / activity schedule**.
8. **Conditions of contract + contract data** — the obligations you accept if you win.
9. **Annexures** — drawings, site information, existing reports, specifications by reference.

### 3.3 The two killer questions

Every pack, always, in this order:

**Q1 — "What kills us before reading?"**
Wrong CIDB designation · not registered on CSD · missed compulsory briefing · late submission · wrong submission channel · unsigned SBD 4 · unpriced item · restricted supplier · bid validity not accepted · no valid tax status.

**Q2 — "What makes us score badly while fully compliant?"**
Thin methodology · irrelevant experience · unverifiable references · under-qualified key personnel · no programme · weak risk treatment · price uncompetitive · low preference points.

These map to Layers 1–2 and Layers 3–4 respectively. Never merge them in your notes: the remedies are completely different. Layer 1–2 problems mean *don't bid or fix immediately*. Layer 3–4 problems mean *invest writing time*.

### 3.4 Vocabulary you must own this week

**bidder · tenderer · organ of state · accounting officer/authority · bid specification committee · bid evaluation committee · bid adjudication committee · responsiveness · material deviation · functionality · threshold · returnable · corrigendum · addendum · compulsory briefing/site meeting · bid validity period · bid/tender security · two-envelope system · pre-qualification criteria · restricted supplier / Register of Tender Defaulters · condonation · award · appeal/objection · debriefing.**

Write your own one-line definition of each in the Tender Bible. Do not copy a dictionary — define it by its *effect on a bid*.

### 3.5 Corrigenda discipline

A corrigendum can move a closing date, change scope, change the CIDB grading, replace a form, or answer clarification questions in a binding way. **Re-download the tender page before every submission.** Build the habit now: log the date you last checked the portal for each live tender.

3. Why is the bid validity period a commercial risk, not just an admin detail?

5. Name six things that cause disqualification before evaluation.
6. What must you do immediately before submitting, regardless of how well you know the pack?
