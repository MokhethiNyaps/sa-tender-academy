## 3. Core concepts

### 3.1 What CIDB is doing

For public-sector construction work, contractors are registered on the **Register of Contractors** and categorised by **class of works** and **grade**. The required designation determines which tenders a contractor may compete for. CIDB stresses that public-sector clients must apply the Register when **preparing, advertising and evaluating** applicable construction tenders.

**Sources:**
- Register of Contractors guide (2026): https://www.cidb.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/guide-to-the-cidb-register-of-contractors.pdf
- Applying the Register of Contractors: https://www.cidb.org.za/clients/procurement-guides/applying-register-of-contractors/
- Register overview: https://www.cidb.org.za/contractors/register-of-contractors/overview/

### 3.2 Reading a designation

Format: **`<grade><class>`**, e.g. `4GB`, `5CE`, `3EB`, `7ME`. Sometimes with a status suffix such as PE (potentially emerging) — confirm the current notation in the CIDB guide as you read it.

- **Grade** = financial capability band → the tender-value limit the contractor may bid up to.
- **Class** = the type of construction work.

So `5CE` = grade 5, civil engineering works.

### 3.3 Tender-value limits by grade

Per the current CIDB guide, the limits shown include:

| Grade | Tender value limit |
|---|---|
| 1 | Lowest band (entry level) |
| 2 | up to R1 million |
| 3 | up to R3 million |
| 4 | up to R6 million |
| 5 | up to R10 million |
| 6 | up to R20 million |
| 7 | up to R60 million |
| 8 | up to R200 million |
| 9 | Unlimited |

**Verify these against the CIDB guide each time you cite them to a client** — limits are periodically revised. Record the version/date of the guide you used in your Tender Bible.

### 3.4 Major classes of works

| Code | Class |
|---|---|
| **GB** | General Building |
| **CE** | Civil Engineering |
| **EB** | Electrical Engineering Works – Building |
| **EP** | Electrical Engineering Works – Infrastructure |
| **ME** | Mechanical Engineering |
| *SB / SC / etc.* | Specialist classes (asphalt, geotechnical, demolition, lifts, fire, landscaping, road markings, structural steel, water/waste treatment, etc.) |

Learn the specialist classes from the CIDB guide's list — do not guess them. Many tenders are won or lost on whether the correct specialist class was required or held.

### 3.5 What a construction advertisement tells you in 3 minutes

From the advertisement alone you should extract:
- Tender number, buyer, framework (PFMA/MFMA → SBD/MBD)
- **Required CIDB designation** (grade + class), and whether "or higher" is stated
- Whether a lower grade is permitted subject to conditions
- Estimated value or value band
- Compulsory site meeting/briefing: date, time, venue, and whether attendance is a **disqualifying** requirement
- Document fee, collection method
- Closing date, time and **exact** submission point
- Preference system indicated (80/20 or 90/10) and any pre-qualification criteria
- Contact person for technical vs administrative queries

### 3.6 The go/no-go moment

CIDB eligibility is the fastest no-go signal in the business. A 30-second check saves a client two weeks. Make it the first thing you do on every construction opportunity:

```
Required:  5CE or higher, active
Client:    4CE, active, CRS ######
Verdict:   NOT ELIGIBLE as sole bidder.
Options:   (a) do not bid  (b) JV with an eligible partner — see Week 6
           (c) check whether an upgrade is realistically achievable before closing
```
