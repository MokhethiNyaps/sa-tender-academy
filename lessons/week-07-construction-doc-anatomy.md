## 1. Why this week matters

A 250-page construction pack is not harder than a 40-page service tender — it is just longer and organised by a convention you have not learned yet. This week you learn the convention, and you learn where your scope ends and a professional's begins.

By Friday you can:

## 3. Core concepts

### 3.1 The standard construction pack structure

Commonly organised into parts such as:

| Part | Typical contents |
|---|---|
| **T1 — Tendering procedures** | Tender notice and invitation; **Standard Conditions of Tender**; tender data |
| **T2 — Returnable documents** | List of returnables; returnable schedules; SBD/MBD forms; certificates |
| **C1 — Agreements & contract data** | Form of offer and acceptance; contract data (part 1 employer, part 2 contractor); guarantees |
| **C2 — Pricing data** | Pricing instructions; **Bill of Quantities** / activity schedule |
| **C3 — Scope of work** | Description of works; specifications; standards; constraints; management requirements |
| **C4 — Site information** | Site conditions, existing services, geotech reports, drawings |

CIDB describes its **Standard Conditions of Tender** as setting the rules from tender invitation through award — read them once properly and you will recognise them in pack after pack.
**Source:** https://www.cidb.org.za/clients/procurement-guides/expressions-of-interest/ · prescripts: https://www.cidb.org.za/clients/procurement-prescripts/cidb-prescripts/

### 3.2 Terminology — conceptual mastery required

You do **not** need to be a Quantity Surveyor. These terms must simply stop frightening you.

| Term | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **BOQ — Bill of Quantities** | Itemised list of work items with units and measured quantities, to be priced by the contractor |
| **Scope of works** | What is to be built/done, its extent and boundaries |
| **Specifications** | The standards/quality the work must meet (national standards, project-specific) |
| **Preliminaries (P&Gs)** | Time- and project-related costs not tied to a measured item: site establishment, supervision, facilities, insurances |
| **Provisional sums** | Money allowed for work not yet fully defined; spent at the employer's instruction |
| **Prime cost sums** | Allowances for items supplied by a nominated source/subcontractor |
| **Rates** | The contractor's price per unit of an item |
| **Quantities** | The measured amount of each item |
| **Site establishment** | Setting up the site: offices, stores, ablutions, fencing, signage, services |
| **Construction programme** | Time plan of activities, durations, dependencies, milestones |
| **Method statement** | How a particular activity will be executed, safely and to specification |
| **Resources** | Labour, plant, materials, subcontractors allocated to the work |
| **Plant and equipment** | Machinery available/committed to the project |
| **Key personnel** | Named individuals whose qualification and experience are evaluated |
| **Cash flow** | Expected value of work over time; often a returnable |
| **Retention** | A percentage of payments withheld as security, released at defined stages |
| **Performance guarantee** | Bank/insurer security for performance, usually a % of contract value |
| **Practical completion** | Works are complete enough to be used for their intended purpose |
| **Defects liability / defects period** | Period after completion during which the contractor fixes defects |
| **Variations** | Instructed changes to scope, with pricing consequences |
| **Extension of time (EOT)** | Additional time granted for qualifying delays |
| **Liquidated damages / penalties** | Pre-agreed amount payable per day of late completion |
| **Contract data** | The project-specific variables that complete the standard conditions of contract |

Add each to the Tender Bible with a one-line "why it matters to a bid" note.

### 3.3 The scope boundary — memorise this table

| Section | Who owns it | Your role |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility & CIDB checks | **You** | Own it entirely |
| Returnables, forms, declarations | **You** | Own it entirely |
| Compliance matrix & submission packaging | **You** | Own it entirely |
| Experience, references, CVs | **You** (from client facts) | Compile, format, verify — never invent |
| Methodology / method statements | **You + contractor** | Interview and draft; contractor approves |
| Programme | **You + contractor** | Draft narrative & structure; contractor sets durations |
| Risk register, quality plan | **You + contractor** | Draft; contractor/specialist confirms |
| H&S documentation | **Safety specialist** | Coordinate, check completeness only |
| BOQ rates & pricing | **Contractor / QS** | Check arithmetic and blanks only |
| Design, drawings, calculations | **Engineer** | Never touch |
| Professional sign-off | **Registered professional** | Never sign |
| Legal interpretation of contract conditions | **Attorney** | Flag risks, do not advise |

Say this table out loud to clients. It protects them and it protects you.

### 3.4 Conditions of contract

You will encounter standard forms of contract used in SA construction (e.g. general conditions of contract for construction works, JBCC, NEC, FIDIC families). This week you only need to:
1. Identify **which** form the pack uses.
2. Find the **contract data** and read the project-specific variables: completion time, penalties per day, retention %, guarantee %, payment terms, defects period, insurance requirements.
3. List the top 10 obligations the client is accepting.

That is Layer 5. It feeds directly into the go/no-go decision.
