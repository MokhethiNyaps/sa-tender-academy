# Week 9 — Method Statements, Programmes and Contractor Translation

> **Assignment volume:** draft **3 contractor methodologies** from real scopes.
> **Running total target:** 51 tenders.

---

## 1. Why this week matters

This is your highest-value skill. A contractor may know exactly how to do the work and be unable to write it down. You interview them and convert operational expertise into an **evaluable response**. You are not pretending to be the engineer — you are the translator.

---

## 2. Learning objectives

By Friday you can:
- Run a structured 60-minute contractor interview that yields a complete methodology.
- Convert a verbal sequence into a method statement, resource plan, QC plan, risk register, programme narrative, environmental controls and H&S approach.
- Build a defensible construction programme narrative.
- Self-score the output against the tender's functionality criteria.

## 3. Core concepts

### 3.1 The worked example

Scope: **rehabilitation of 2 km of municipal road.**

The contractor explains it verbally:

> establish site → traffic accommodation → setting out → scarify → earthworks → layerworks → compaction → surfacing → road markings → snagging

That sequence is the skeleton of everything. From it you produce:

1. **Method statement** — each activity: what, how, with what plant, by whom, to what standard, with what hold point.
2. **Resource plan** — labour teams, plant list with numbers, materials sources, subcontractors.
3. **Quality-control plan** — tests per layer (density, thickness, levels), frequency, standard, who signs off.
4. **Risk register** — traffic/public safety, utilities strikes, weather, material supply, community disruption, access.
5. **Programme narrative** — logic, durations, dependencies, critical path, float, weather allowance.
6. **Environmental controls** — dust, noise, spoil, spill containment, water, rehabilitation of borrow/stockpile areas.
7. **H&S approach** — the tender's OHS specification requirements, site-specific plan, appointments, inductions, PPE, traffic accommodation safety.

### 3.2 The contractor interview script

Run it in this order; record it; never fill gaps yourself.

**A. Understanding**
1. Have you done this exact type of work before? Where, when, what value?
2. What is different or difficult about *this* site?
3. What would you worry about on day one?

**B. Sequence**
4. Walk me from site handover to final handover, activity by activity.
5. What happens before each activity can start?
6. Which activities can overlap?

**C. Per activity (loop)**
7. How exactly do you do it?
8. What plant? How many? Owned or hired?
9. What team size and which trades?
10. What material, from which supplier, what lead time?
11. What standard/specification governs it?
12. What test or inspection proves it was done right? Who signs?
13. How long does it take at what production rate?

**D. Constraints**
14. Traffic/public/access/working hours?
15. Existing services? Who locates them?
16. Weather-sensitive activities and your allowance?
17. Community/local labour expectations?

**E. People and risk**
18. Who is the site agent, foreman, safety officer? Qualifications?
19. Top five things that have gone wrong on similar jobs, and what you now do differently?
20. What do you need from the client/engineer to stay on programme?

Anything unanswered goes into the draft as `MISSING — CLIENT TO PROVIDE`.

### 3.3 Method statement format

```
ACTIVITY 4 — LAYERWORKS
Purpose:            <what this achieves in the works>
Preconditions:      <preceding activity complete, approvals, tests passed>
Method:             <step 1..n, concrete and sequential>
Plant:              <list with quantities>
Labour:             <team composition>
Materials:          <source, standard, delivery>
Standards:          <specification clause, national standard>
Quality control:    <test, frequency, acceptance criteria, sign-off>
Hold points:        <where work stops for inspection>
Safety:             <hazards and controls>
Environmental:      <controls>
Duration:           <days, production rate assumption>
Responsible:        <named person / role>
Output/evidence:    <records produced>
```

### 3.4 Programme narrative

A Gantt chart alone scores poorly. Add a narrative that states:
- the logic of the sequence and why it suits this site
- the production rates underpinning durations
- the critical path and what protects it
- float and where it sits
- weather/access/approval allowances
- resource loading over time, and how it matches the cash-flow schedule
- the milestones that match the contract data's dates

### 3.5 Common failures to avoid

- Generic methodology that could describe any road anywhere (scores near zero).
- Durations with no production-rate basis.
- Plant lists with no quantities or ownership status.
- Quality plans that restate the specification without saying who tests, when and to what frequency.
- Risk registers full of generic risks with generic mitigations.
- H&S sections that promise compliance without naming appointments and documents.
- A programme that contradicts the contract's completion date.

## 6. Self-test (closed book)

1. What five things must every activity in a method statement specify?
2. Why does a Gantt chart without a narrative score badly?
3. What is a hold point?
4. Give three site-specific risks for a 2 km urban road rehabilitation.
5. What do you write when the contractor cannot tell you a production rate?
6. Where is the line between your work and the engineer's on a method statement?
