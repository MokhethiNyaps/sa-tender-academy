## 3. Core concepts

### 3.1 COIDA and the Letter of Good Standing

The Department of Employment and Labour explains that employers using contractors should obtain a **Compensation Fund Letter of Good Standing** from those contractors, demonstrating registration and that assessment payments are up to date.

What it proves: the contractor is registered with the Compensation Fund and its assessments are current.
What to check: entity name matches the bidder exactly · expiry date vs closing date **and** likely award date · lead time to obtain or renew.

**Source:** https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Pages/Compensation-Fund--obligations-of-the-employer-.aspx

### 3.2 The conditional compliance universe

You may encounter any of these:

**COIDA · UIF · NHBRC · professional registrations · trade certifications · PSIRA · municipal accounts (rates clearance / no arrears) · insurances · performance guarantees · health-and-safety documentation · sector accreditations**

> **Never assume all of these are mandatory universally. The specific tender decides.**

### 3.3 Conditional logic — the distinction that matters

**Wrong:** "Every construction company must submit NHBRC for every tender."

**Right:** "NHBRC may matter here because this is homebuilding."

NHBRC states that anyone in the business of building homes is required to register and renew membership annually. So the trigger is the *nature of the work*, not the word "construction."
**Source:** https://www.nhbrc.org.za/why-register/

Build a trigger table in your Tender Bible:

| Document | Trigger | Where the tender says so |
|---|---|---|
| Letter of Good Standing (COIDA) | Employer with employees; nearly all construction packs | Returnables list / conditions |
| NHBRC registration | Home building work | Scope + returnables |
| PSIRA | Security services element | Scope + returnables |
| Professional registration (ECSA/SACPCMP/SACAP etc.) | Named professional roles required | Key personnel criteria |
| Trade certifications | Specified trades (e.g. electrical CoC capability) | Specification |
| Municipal accounts / rates clearance | Frequently required by municipalities | MBD / returnables |
| Insurances (CAR, public liability, SASRIA) | Contract data requirement | C1 contract data |
| Performance guarantee | Above a stated value threshold | Contract data |
| Sector B-BBEE (construction sector code) | Where the tender specifies the sector code | Preference clause |

Every row must cite **this tender's** clause before you request the document from a client.

### 3.4 Health and safety documentation

Typical H&S items in a construction pack: OHS specification issued by the client; a required site-specific **health and safety plan**; baseline risk assessment; appointments (construction manager, safety officer, first aiders, etc.); proof of competence; incident statistics; letter of good standing; sometimes a priced H&S item in the preliminaries.

**Your role:** coordinate and check completeness against the tender's list; identify who must produce each item; ensure the H&S plan is site-specific and aligned to your methodology. **A safety specialist produces and signs it.** Never draft a risk assessment as if qualified.

### 3.6 The expiry calendar

Build a simple calendar of every expiring document per client, with reminders at 60/30/14 days. Most construction bids that die administratively die on an expiry date someone forgot.
